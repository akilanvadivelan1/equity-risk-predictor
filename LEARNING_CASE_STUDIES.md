## 1. Target (TGT), 2022

**Card summary:** How a few words about excess inventory showed up before the worst trading day in 35 years.

**Category:** Retail

### Full article

In early 2022, Target looked healthy on the surface. Sales were still growing, and the stock had spent the prior year near record highs. If you only watched the price, nothing seemed wrong.

The words told a quieter story. In its annual report, Target's risk language grew more specific about excess inventory, supply chain disruption, and rising costs. The calm, repeated boilerplate from earlier years gave way to sharper, more concrete warnings. These were not dramatic headlines. They were small shifts in wording, the kind that are easy to skim past.

Then the numbers caught up. On May 18, 2022, Target reported first quarter results and cut its operating margin outlook from over 8 percent to about 6 percent, pointing to the very inventory and cost pressures it had been describing. The stock fell 24.9 percent in a single session to about 161 dollars, its worst day since 1987, erasing roughly 25 billion dollars in market value.

**What this shows.** The financial results still looked fine right up until they did not. The wording had already turned less confident. Reading both together, the words and the numbers, gave a fuller picture than watching the stock price alone. This is an observation, not a prediction. We are exploring whether the patterns academic research describes actually show up in real companies, and Target 2022 is one of the clearest examples.

## 2. Southwest Airlines (LUV), 2022

**Card summary:** An operational risk the company disclosed for years, until a winter storm turned it into a meltdown.

**Category:** Airlines

### Full article

Some risks are not hidden at all. They sit in plain view, disclosed year after year, until people stop paying attention to them.

For years, Southwest's filings flagged its reliance on aging technology and crew scheduling systems as an operational risk. The warning was consistent and public. Because it repeated with little change, it read as routine, the kind of standing caution that every airline includes. Familiarity made it easy to ignore.

In December 2022, a severe winter storm overwhelmed those exact systems. The crew scheduling software could not keep up, and the airline was forced into thousands of cancellations, stranding travelers over the holidays. What had been a quiet, repeated line in a filing became a very public operational crisis. The stock fell about 15.6 percent that month as the meltdown played out.

**What this shows.** A risk a company repeats every year is easy to tune out, but repetition does not make it harmless. Tracking which disclosed risks are most exposed, rather than dismissing the ones that never change, can help decide which deserve a second look. This is an observation, not a prediction. The warning was there for anyone reading closely.

## 3. SVB Financial, Silicon Valley Bank (SIVB), 2022 to 2023

**Card summary:** A bond portfolio and a concentrated deposit base, both disclosed, both overlooked, until the bank failed in days.

**Category:** Banking

### Full article

Silicon Valley Bank's collapse felt sudden, but the ingredients were disclosed well before the end.

The bank's filings described a large bond portfolio that was exposed to rising interest rates, and a deposit base heavily concentrated in technology startups. Neither of these was a secret. Both were stated plainly in its reports. On their own, each looked like a normal line item. Together, they were a fragile combination waiting for the wrong conditions.

Through 2022, as interest rates rose, the value of that bond portfolio fell, and the stock drifted down from around 559 dollars to the low 200s. The slow decline did not alarm most investors. Then it moved fast. In March 2023, the bank sold bonds at a loss and tried to raise capital. Its concentrated depositors, many of them connected startups, rushed to withdraw at the same time. The stock crashed from about 268 dollars to 106 dollars on March 9, and trading was halted on March 10 as regulators closed the bank.

**What this shows.** The two ingredients of the failure, interest rate exposure and depositor concentration, were both in the filings long before the collapse. The story moved slowly for a year and then very quickly in a matter of days. This is an observation, not a prediction. It is a reminder that the most important risks are sometimes the ones a company states plainly and everyone reads past.
