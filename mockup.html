Two ways to see risk

The numbers tell you where a company has been. The risk warnings hint at where it's going. Most investors only look at one. We read both.

The Words · looks at the future

Risk-factor language

What companies are legally required to disclose — and how their tone changes when trouble is coming.

  • Brand-new risks that weren't there last year
  • Softened boilerplate turning specific & urgent
  • Negative & uncertainty word density rising
  • Risks quietly dropped or downplayed
  • Shifts the market hasn't priced in yet

Academic Backing: "Lazy Prices" (2020)

Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen found that a strategy buying companies whose filings barely changed — and selling those whose filings changed a lot — earned about 22% per year in abnormal returns. Textual changes predict future problems, yet almost nobody reads these documents.

Why This Works

Companies are legally required to disclose their risks. They often know about problems before the numbers show it — but bury the warnings in hundreds of pages of dense legal language. A system that reads all of it, every year, and measures what changed has a real edge over investors who only watch the stock price.
