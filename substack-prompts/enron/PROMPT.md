# Writing Prompt: The Enron Post

> Use this prompt with any AI writing assistant, or as a writing brief for yourself.
> It is tuned to match Akilan Vadivelan's established Substack voice.

---

You are helping me write a Substack post in the established voice of Akilan Vadivelan, a rising high-school senior who writes about how companies build, break, and reinvent business models. Study and match this voice exactly.

## Voice and style rules (follow strictly)

- Open with a paradox or an unsettling fact that creates tension. One or two short lines. No throat-clearing.
- Use short, declarative sentences. Deliberate fragments are allowed for rhythm ("Enron was a gas company. Then it wasn't.").
- Treat numbers as drama. Every big number must be given meaning, not just stated.
- Pause to teach the finance concept in plain English using an everyday analogy a smart teenager would get. Introduce each one in its own labeled block: "Concept 1: ...", "Concept 2: ...".
- Keep one clear through-line question that you pose early and answer by the end.
- Reading level: sharp but accessible, roughly 9th to 11th grade. No jargon left unexplained.
- Tone: curious and clear-eyed, not preachy. Explain the trick, then explain why it was wrong.

## The subject

Enron, and the broader theme of how companies have weaponized finance to deceive rather than create value. This post opens a turn in my series: from finance used cleverly to finance used to do wrong.

## The concepts to teach through the Enron story (explain each simply, with an analogy)

1. **Mark-to-market accounting** — booking the entire future expected profit of a deal today, as if it already happened. (Analogy: counting the full value of a 20-year lemonade contract as income the day you sign it, before selling a single cup.)
2. **Special Purpose Entities / off-balance-sheet debt** — hiding losses and debt in separate shell companies so the parent looks healthy. (Analogy: shoving your mess into a friend's garage so your own room looks clean.)
3. **Why the stock had to keep rising** — the trap where the illusion only survives if the number keeps going up, and what happens when it can't.

## The through-line question to pose and answer

How did a company that was mostly an illusion become, briefly, the 7th most valuable company in America, and why was its collapse inevitable from the start?

## Structure

- Hook (the paradox).
- What Enron actually was, and what it claimed to be.
- The through-line question.
- The concept blocks, each tied to the real Enron story.
- The unraveling (2001), told as the moment the math caught up.
- A short closing reflection on the real lesson: finance is a tool, and the same tools that build value can manufacture a mirage. End with a line that lands.

## Also include at the end

A short, honest note that this is the first in a run of posts about finance used to deceive, naming one or two other cases I might cover next (for example, the 2008 mortgage-backed-securities collapse, or WorldCom).

## Length and accuracy

Keep it to roughly 1,000 to 1,400 words. Fact-check the core numbers and dates (Enron's peak valuation, the 2001 bankruptcy, Arthur Andersen's role) and keep them accurate.
